The story in one minute

Quantization

Rounding a model's weights to 8 or 4 bits makes it cheap to run. It costs some accuracy: almost nothing at 8-bit, a few points at 4-bit, and more for small models.

Self-improvement

A model answers each question several times, trusts the answer most attempts agree on, and trains on it. No human labels. It only works if agreement is a reliable sign of being right.

The gap

Quantization papers score one answer at a time. Self-improvement papers run at full precision. Almost nobody checks whether rounding damages the agreement signal itself.

Our answer so far: at 4-bit a small model is right less often and sure less often, but when it is sure it is just as trustworthy. We therefore expect a compressed model to improve itself more slowly, not to be misled by bad labels. This is provisional: one quantizer, some small question sets, and no training loop yet.

    How the cards were made, and what was corrected

    Each paper was looked up on arXiv (title, authors, versions, comments field) and its venue was checked on a proceedings or venue page where one exists. The full text was retrieved from arXiv HTML, ar5iv or the ACL Anthology and the method and numbers were taken from it. Each card says how much was read. Numbers are copied from the papers' own tables and text; where a paper contradicts itself the card says so.

    The one-line idea, the "how we use it" label and the notes on each side of our question are this project's reading of the papers, not claims by their authors. The "what we add" column comes from the stored runs described in the dossier and is provisional.
