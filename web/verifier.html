Experiment D: Verifier Reliability Analysis

Does quantization break the reliability of self-judgment in small language models?

Home Original Proposal Problem & Datasets Phase 1: Generation Phase 2: Verifier Next Steps

Executive Research Summary

In direct response to the project plan's core question—"Does compression break the reliability of self-judgment?"—we evaluated candidate SLMs by comparing their 16-bit self-verification performance directly against their 8-bit and 4-bit states.

  • The "Delta Illusion": We cannot measure verifier survival by Selection Delta alone. If a 4-bit verifier yields a higher Delta than 16-bit, it is often because the 4-bit generator collapsed, making it artificially easy for the verifier to beat the baseline. We must track degradation via False Positive Rate (FPR) and MCC instead.
  • 8-bit is Highly Robust: Across almost all models, 8-bit quantization preserves verification capabilities exceptionally well, showing statistically insignificant reliability loss compared to 16-bit.
  • 4-bit Causes Measurable Degradation: Aggressive 4-bit quantization consistently degrades verifier calibration (FPRs rise, MCCs drop). However, for robust models like Qwen3.5-2B, it does not universally destroy the capability, leaving them viable for self-improvement loops.
  • Architecture Matters Most: Models like Gemma-3 and Llama-3.2 exhibit catastrophic failure (>85% FPR) even at full 16-bit precision, making them fundamentally unsafe for unsupervised self-training regardless of quantization.

1. Evaluation Methodology & Core Metrics

To accurately assess quantization damage, we track these critical metrics (comparing 8-bit/4-bit against the 16-bit baseline):

Safety Risk: False Positive Rate (FPR)

Goal: LOWER is better

Formula: $\frac{FP}{FP+TN}$

The rate at which the verifier incorrectly approves a flawed trace. A rising FPR as precision drops means the model is becoming falsely confident in incorrect logic—polluting the self-training dataset.

Discrimination: MCC

Goal: HIGHER is better

Formula: $\frac{TP\cdot TN-FP\cdot FN}{\sqrt{(TP+FP)(TP+FN)(TN+FP)(TN+FN)}}$

A balanced metric of verification quality robust to class imbalance. A drop in MCC relative to 16-bit indicates a degradation in the verifier's fundamental classification ability.

Selection Utility: Delta

Goal: HIGHER is better (but with caveats)

Formula: $Acc_{guided} - Acc_{generator}$

Measures overall verifier impact. However, Delta is deceptive for measuring quantization damage because the generator baseline itself shifts during quantization.

4. Next Steps: Proceeding to Experiment B

This phase yields a clear "Outcome 1/3" hybrid based on our project plan: agreement and self-judgment reliability degrade measurably under 4-bit compression (FPR climbs), but they do not hit a floor state in the Qwen family, while 8-bit acts as a highly robust middle-ground.

Because the verifier does not catastrophically fail at 4-bit, we have clearance to proceed to Experiment B. We will run a complete QLoRA self-improvement loop (Generate → Verify → Filter → Train) using the Qwen3.5 architecture. By forcing the 4-bit model to train on its own verifier-selected data, we will determine if the 31-44% FPR noise rate is low enough to allow self-improvement, or if that degradation causes compounding training collapse over multiple iterations.
