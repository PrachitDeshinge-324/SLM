Executive Summary: How Self-Training Unfolds Across 3 Stages

In label-free self-training, the model generates its own reasoning traces, filters them by consensus agreement, and retrains itself. To see what changes each round in Small Language Models (SLMs), we track three stages:

flowchart LR A["500 Pool Questions
GSM8K Training Split"] --> B["Stage 1: Generator
Sample 8 traces per question
(4,000 total solutions)"] B --> C["Stage 2: Verifier / Filter
Select confident subset
(≥ 6 of 8 agreement)"] C --> D["LoRA Training
SFT on harvested traces"] D --> E["Stage 3: Test Set
Evaluate on unseen test set
(100 questions)"] D -.->|"Next Round Rollout"| B

Core Intuition in 1 Minute

  • Stage 1 (Generator, 0.8B 4-bit vote arm): One sample on the pool rises 38.0 → 46.5 → 48.0%, while the majority vote of its 8 samples stays flat (56.0 → 58.2 → 56.4%). Part of the one-sample rise is finishing (cut-offs 17.1 → 7.4%) and part is better finished answers (45.8 → 51.9% right when finished). At 16-bit, the gain on unseen pool questions is all finishing.
  • Stage 2 (Verifier / Filter): More questions reach 6 of 8 agreement ($123 \rightarrow 197 \rightarrow 234$), but the share of wrong labels triples from 4.1% to 13.2%. Rising agreement is therefore not evidence of accuracy.
  • Stage 3 (Held-Out Test Set, 100 questions): One sample rises in Round 1 (+6.6 [+2.6, +10.5]), and later rounds show no clear change. Every vote change on the test set stays inside the ±8-point re-run noise.

Falling label quality is measured, but we have not shown that it causes any decline. No difference between the three arms holds across training seeds. Each cell here is one run.

2. Real Examples of What Each Stage Does

These are real questions and samples from the 0.8B 4-bit pool runs, shortened with "…". They were chosen to show each case clearly, so they illustrate the stages and say nothing about how common each case is. Reference answers appear here only for scoring.

Case A: A Right Label (Agreement & Correct) Stage 2 Success

Question: "James buys 5 packs of beef that are 4 pounds each. The price of beef is $5.50 per pound. How much did he pay?" (Reference answer = 110)

Round 0 Samples (8 Samples)

All 8 samples reach 110. One of them:

5 packs x 4 pounds/pack = 20 pounds of beef
20 pounds x $5.50/pound = $110.00
James paid 110 dollars. #### 110
Vote: 8 of 8 agree on 110, above the 6-of-8 threshold. Kept for training.
What the Next Round Learns

The LoRA adapter trains on these solutions. At 0.8B 4-bit, 118 of the 123 labels kept in Round 0 are right.

Result: Most kept labels in Round 0 are correct (95.9%).
Case B: A Wrong Label (Shared Misreading) Stage 2 Failure

Question: "Jeremy listened to five fewer songs yesterday than today. Yesterday, he listened to nine songs. How many songs did Jeremy listen to in two days?" (Reference answer = 23)

Round 0 Samples (8 Samples)

6 of 8 samples misread the comparison and get 13. The other 2 get the right answer, 23:

Since he listened to five fewer songs today,
he listened to 9 - 5 = 4 songs today.
9 (yesterday) + 4 (today) = 13 songs.
#### 13
Vote: 6 of 8 agree on 13, which meets the threshold. Kept for training.
What the Next Round Learns

The filter has no reference answers, so high agreement is all it sees. The solutions reaching 13 go into training, and the 2 right solutions are dropped.

Measured: 5 of 123 kept labels are wrong in Round 0, and 31 of 234 in the Round 2 pool run. We have not shown that these wrong labels cause any decline.
Case C: Finishing Inside the Token Limit Stage 1 Change

Question: "A farmer has twice as many pigs as cows, and 4 more cows than goats. If the farmer has 56 animals total, how many goats does he have?" (Reference answer = 11)

Round 0 (Untrained Model)

5 of 8 samples are cut off here. This one is still working when it reaches the limit, as most cut-off samples are:

… Let me re-read the equation setup.
Equation: C + P + G = 56
C + 2C + (C + 4) = 56
…
Total = 13 + [CUT OFF AT 512 TOKENS]
Scoring: Hit the token limit, so it counts as wrong.
Round 1 (After One Round of Self-Training)

2 of 8 samples are cut off. This one sets up the same problem and finishes:

"4 more cows than goats" implies: C = G + 4
From this, we can solve for G: G = C - 4
…
[Finished in 384 tokens, answer 11]
Scoring: Finished within 512 tokens with the right answer.

Key Takeaway: Learning to finish is a large part of the Pass@1 rise from 38% to 48%, but not all of it at 4-bit. From Round 0 to Round 2, average length falls 324 → 282 tokens and cut-offs fall 17.1 → 7.4%, while right-when-finished rises 45.8 → 51.9%. On 377 pool questions it never trained on, the Round 1 model gains +3.9 [+1.6, +6.3] when finished at 4-bit. At 16-bit the same gain is −1.1 [−3.6, +1.4] on 330 questions. This comparison is one run at 0.8B, so treat it as a hypothesis.

3. Literature Context: How Our Findings Align with Prior Work

No paper we read reports all three stages per round. The early jump and later plateau match published results, and ReST-EM also sees training performance keep rising while test does not. The main difference is the vote: ReST-EM, LMSI, MACA and SRT report vote gains, and ours does not move. Values come from extracts of each paper, so check them against the PDFs before citing.

Paper & Model Method Reported Trajectory Comparison to Our Findings
Qi et al. (2026)
Gemma-1B & 4B, Knights and Knaves		 Iterative Self-Verification (DPO) Gemma-1B barely moves (7.8% → 8.4%). On 4B, Pass@1 rises 31.0 → 40.7% while Pass@32 stays flat (78.0% → 77.4%). Partial match: Pass@1 rises in both, and small models gain least. It is a different task, and it does not report label quality per round.
LMSI (Huang et al., 2022)
PaLM-540B; UL2		 Majority Vote Self-Training PaLM-540B GSM8K vote 74.4% → 82.1%. UL2 gains much less, which the authors attribute to model size. LMSI ran only one round. Differs on the vote: its vote rises and ours does not. It supports the size effect, but it says nothing about later rounds.
ReST-EM (Singh et al., 2023)
PaLM-2 on MATH & APPS		 Generate, filter by a gold-answer reward, then fine-tune Training performance rises linearly while test does not. Small test gains after iteration 1 on MATH. APPS regresses at iteration 2; the authors suspect overfitting on a small set of problems. Matches the shape: train keeps rising and test plateaus. Its labels are gold-filtered, so this happens without any wrong labels.
SRT (Shafayat et al., 2025)
Four base models, including Qwen2.5-Math-7B		 Online RL on the model's own majority vote Vote quality rises early, then all four base models collapse with prolonged training. After 1,200 steps Qwen2.5-Math-7B outputs a constant answer. Different setting: online RL counted in steps, not rounds. Our runs show no collapse, but both show agreement can rise without accuracy.
MACA (Samanta et al., 2025)
Qwen-2B & Llama-3B, debate of 3 clones		 Multi-agent debate consensus GSM8K debate vote: Qwen-2B 49.6 → 60.8 → 62.2 → 62.6%; Llama-3B 65.6 → 72.0 → 74.6 → 70.8%. Big first jump, then diminishing returns. Partial match: the first jump and plateau match, but its vote rises and ours does not. It uses debate, a 256-token cap and 1,500 training questions.
